# Scene mode: how to draw so it does not look like slides

Everything here is code-only: SVG in React, no image assets.

## What makes a frame read as illustration

| Slide-like | Illustration |
|---|---|
| flat single-colour shapes | a base, a shaded side, a lit edge (three tones minimum per form) |
| icons floating on a background | objects standing on a ground, casting a shadow, overlapping each other |
| one layer | 4–6 depth layers: sky, far trees, building, mid props, people, foreground framing |
| seven hand-placed dots | detail generated by a seeded loop: tiles, leaves, windows, pores, crowd |
| a generic "building" | *that* building: its roof pitch, window count, lettering (from `refs.md`) |
| a static picture with a text fade | something is always moving: clouds, leaves, flags, a walk, a camera push |
| portrait busts | figures in the space, doing the thing the narration says |

Techniques, with where to see them:

- **Form by gradient and two overlays.** Wall = gradient + a shadow band under the eave. `NiasBuilding`.
- **Texture by pattern or one static filter.** Roof tiles are an SVG `<pattern>`; stone and wood grain are
  `feTurbulence` filters that never animate. `Defs` in `UnairScene.tsx`, `Defs2` in `UnairParts.tsx`.
- **Seeded procedure.** `rng(seed)` gives the same "random" every frame. Use it for anything you would otherwise
  copy-paste: foliage blobs, book spines, pizza toppings, pores in stone. `Foliage`, `buildPizza`.
- **Relief.** For carved or embossed things draw each part three times: dark copy offset down-right, the fill, a
  thin light stroke offset up-left. `Cut` in `UnairArca.tsx`.
- **Rigs.** People are bones with joints (two-bone IK for arms, a sine for the stride), not one path.
  `ik` in `PizzaScene.tsx`, `Walker` in `UnairParts.tsx`.
- **Parallax.** Far layers move less than near ones when the camera travels. `par()` in `PizzaScene.tsx`.
- **Camera.** One `<g transform="translate(cx cy) scale(z) translate(-x -y)">` around the whole shot; ease a slow
  push through every shot so no frame is frozen.
- **Simulation when a drawn fake reads wrong.** The veil is a 1,409-point Verlet cloth dropped on a collision
  shape and pulled by its hem; the whole run is computed once and cached. `UnairCloth.tsx`.
- **Real geometry for real places.** The map of Java is longitude / latitude points projected flat. `UnairMap.tsx`.

## Two clocks

`Film` gives every shot two numbers:

- `u`, the **beat clock**. Timings like "boards go up at 230" are written against it. With a voice-over, the shot's
  `warp` stretches and squeezes `u` so each beat lands on its word.
- `a`, the **ambient clock**: real frames. Pass it to anything that must keep its own pace: `Flag`, `Foliage`,
  `Palm`, `Cloud`, `Walker` (leg cycle), blinking. Otherwise retiming makes flags wave in slow motion.

A physics run must play at real speed: keep that stretch of the `warp` 1:1 (see the unveil shot in `UnairFilm.tsx`).

## Mistakes already made here (check for each)

| What the user saw | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "It looks like PowerPoint" | spec mode, or shots that are text on a tinted panel | scene mode; a subject doing something in every shot |
| Characters "too simple" | silhouettes / busts | draw skin, hair, clothes, a pose; put them in the scene |
| People walk too fast | stride cycle of 0.5 s | one full stride ≈ 1 s (`sin(t * 0.105)` at 60 fps), and slow the travel to match |
| People pile up at the door | every walker ends on the same point and stays | stagger starts, fade each one out as it passes through |
| Desk seen from the front, person from the side | two viewpoints in one drawing | choose one elevation per shot and draw everything in it |
| Screen faces the viewer while the person faces sideways | same | show the screen edge-on, then cut to an over-the-shoulder shot |
| Glasses "bugged" from behind | drew a lens where only the temple tip shows | think what is visible from that angle |
| Pen floats above the signature | pen path and ink path were separate formulas | sample the ink path by arc length and put the nib on it |
| Lines across the front of a gable | ridge caps drawn after (over) the gable | draw order = depth order; clip repeated detail where something stands in front |
| Fallen cloth lies "under" the stage | stage drawn after the cloth | draw order again; tilt the projection so nearer is lower |
| Cloth flips like an umbrella | rope tied to the top centre | tie it where a person would: the hem on the pulling side |
| Cloth "deflates" | scale animation instead of motion | simulate, or at least move along a path with a believable cause |
| Applause too fast, arms straight up | high frequency, unbent arms, everyone in sync | 0.8 s per clap, bent elbows, per-person phase, only ~⅔ take part |
| A "spin" transition felt odd | fake 3-D turn in 2-D | plain dissolve; keep one transition family for the film |
| Map shaped like a leaf | outline drawn from memory | project real coordinates |
| Voice-over cramped | recording played straight through | cut at pauses, one passage per shot, 0.5 s before and ~1 s after |
| Labels cut off / hidden / overlapping | never opened the still | open the still |

## Checklist for every sheet

1. Is each real object recognisable against its reference photo?
2. Any text clipped by the frame, hidden behind something, or overlapping other text?
3. Is the caption clear of the action (bottom 140 px landscape, bottom 320 px portrait)?
4. Draw order: is anything in front that should be behind?
5. One viewpoint per shot?
6. Do people stand on the ground, at a plausible size for their distance?
7. Mid-action frames: does the in-between pose make physical sense?
8. Empty areas: more than a third of the frame with nothing in it?
9. First and last frame of each shot: is the dissolve landing on something sensible?
10. Does it still look like the same film as the previous shot (palette, line weight, grade)?

## Limits to be honest about

- Faces and creatures drawn in code stay stylised; say so rather than promising realism.
- Stills do not prove motion. You have not watched the video; the user has to.
- Anything without a reference is invented. Name it.
